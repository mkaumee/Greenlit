KOPITIAM NIGHTS
A short film

FADE IN:

INT. KOPITIAM - GEORGE TOWN - NIGHT

Ceiling fans turn slowly. Marble-topped tables, mismatched
rattan chairs. A brass kerosene lamp hangs over the counter,
throwing everything sideways.

RAZAK (60s), in a faded batik shirt, sets down four glasses of
teh tarik. His hands are steady. They have not always been.

Behind him, a wooden birdcage. The merbok inside is silent.

                    RAZAK
          Forty years I pull this tea. Forty
          years the same four cups.

MEI LING (30s) sits across from him with a leather ledger open,
a fountain pen resting in the gutter.

                    MEI LING
          The bank does not care about the tea,
          uncle.

She slides the ledger toward him. He does not look at it.

                    RAZAK
          My father built this counter by hand.

He remembers his father's watch, the weight of it, the way it
caught the light. He has not thought of it in years.

                    MEI LING
          I know.

INT. KOPITIAM - STOREROOM - CONTINUOUS

Shelves of enamel plates. Sacks of coffee beans. A brass
kerosene lamp, twin to the one out front, unlit and dusty.

Razak lifts a songkok from a shelf and turns it over in his
hands. Moth-eaten. He puts it back.

The room smells of rain and old smoke.

EXT. ARMENIAN STREET - NIGHT

Rain. A motorcycle passes somewhere off-screen, unseen.

Razak stands under the awning with a cigarette he does not
light. Mei Ling joins him.

                    MEI LING
          There is a buyer.

                    RAZAK
          There is always a buyer.

INT. KOPITIAM - LATER

Razak takes down the birdcage and sets it on the counter. He
opens the door of it. The merbok does not move.

He picks up one of the teh tarik glasses and throws it at the
wall mirror behind the counter.

The mirror SHATTERS. Glass across the marble.

Mei Ling does not flinch.

                    RAZAK
          Now there is something to sell.

He sweeps the pieces with a rattan broom, slowly, into a
tin dustpan.

                    MEI LING
          Uncle.

                    RAZAK
          Write it in your book.

She closes the ledger. The fountain pen rolls off the table and
she catches it without looking.

EXT. KOPITIAM - DAWN

The shutters are down. A hand-painted signboard above them,
paint flaking: HOCK SENG KOPI, EST. 1961.

The merbok is singing.

FADE OUT.

THE END
